# Target Job Profile

Source: inferred from job positions the user applied to, interviewed for, or said they would like to work in. The attached examples include Technology Community Manager, Tech Lead, Engineering Manager, Technical Expert, Squad Leader, AI Implementation Leader, Product Owner, Product Owner AI, and Tech Manager roles.

Important interpretation rule: job titles in technology can be noisy or disconnected from the real work. Evaluate compatibility by responsibilities, scope, operating model, and required capabilities before relying on the title.

## Target Roles

Primary role families:

- Tech Manager
- Engineering Manager
- Software Engineering Manager
- Technology Manager
- Tech Lead with strong people, delivery, transformation, or stakeholder scope
- Squad Leader
- Agile Delivery Lead / Agile Delivery Manager for technology squads
- AI Implementation Leader
- AI Adoption / AI Transformation Lead
- Technology Community Manager

Secondary role families:

- Product Owner or Product Manager for technical, AI, chatbot, automation, platform, or data-driven products.
- Technical Expert when the role includes leadership, project coordination, stakeholder influence, delivery, architecture, or transformation.
- Digital Transformation Lead.
- Innovation / Technology Strategy Lead.
- Community or enablement roles when they are connected to technology strategy, technical communities, AI adoption, workshops, knowledge sharing, and measurable business impact.

Roles to evaluate carefully:

- Pure hands-on developer roles with little leadership, strategy, facilitation, or stakeholder influence.
- Pure Scrum Master roles without technology leadership, product impact, or engineering management scope.
- Pure Product Owner roles disconnected from technology, AI, digital products, or engineering teams.
- Pure project coordination roles with low autonomy, low strategic scope, or mostly administrative PMO work.
- Deep machine learning research roles requiring academic ML, model training, or advanced data science as the core activity.

## Seniority and Scope

Desired seniority:

- Mid-senior to senior leadership roles where the user can combine technical background, people leadership, agile delivery, AI adoption, and business translation.
- Roles that allow ownership of squads, communities, product/technology initiatives, roadmaps, KPIs, delivery health, engineering practices, or transformation programs.

Minimum acceptable scope:

- Clear responsibility for a squad, product, technology initiative, AI implementation cycle, community program, or delivery stream.
- Direct influence on stakeholders, priorities, metrics, and team ways of working.

Preferred scope:

- Multiple squads or cross-functional teams.
- Leadership of engineering or technology communities.
- Coaching Tech Leads, developing leaders, mentoring teams, or improving team maturity.
- Acting as a bridge between business, technology, product, and executives.
- Running workshops, discovery, process mapping, prioritization, and transformation initiatives.

Less attractive scope:

- Roles focused only on ticket execution.
- Roles where the user is only an intermediary without ownership of outcomes.
- Roles that require mostly status reporting without strategic influence, team development, product thinking, or technical decision participation.

## Industries and Domains

Preferred or attractive domains:

- Technology consulting and digital transformation.
- AI adoption, automation, LLMs, agents, and no-code/low-code enablement.
- Digital products, platforms, marketplaces, channels, apps, portals, and customer-facing systems.
- Retail, beauty, consumer ecosystem, stores/franchise technology, B2B/B2C products.
- Financial services, insurance, fintech, banking technology, payments, and regulated digital products, when the role includes transformation or product/technology impact.
- Real estate technology and scalable product platforms.
- Companies with startup mindset, fast growth, ambiguity, experimentation, and measurable impact.

Acceptable domains:

- Corporate technology environments where the role has autonomy, innovation, stakeholder access, and transformation scope.
- Internal platforms, enterprise systems, and digital channels if the work has visible business value.

Domains to evaluate carefully:

- Highly bureaucratic environments with little room for experimentation.
- Roles dominated by compliance, governance, or reporting without meaningful technology change.

## Work Model

Known signals from liked/applied roles:

- Remote roles are attractive.
- Hybrid in Sao Paulo can be acceptable when the role is strong and strategically aligned.
- Occasional client visits can be acceptable when connected to consulting, stakeholder alignment, discovery, or transformation.

Unknown or missing:

- Exact location constraint.
- Maximum onsite frequency.
- Travel tolerance.
- Contract type preference.
- Compensation expectations.

Treat work model as a strong fit factor only when the job post clearly states remote, hybrid, onsite, travel, or location expectations.

## Skills to Emphasize

Must-use or high-value skills:

- People management and leadership of agile technology teams.
- Technical leadership based on previous software development experience.
- Agile delivery with Scrum, Kanban, flow metrics, DORA metrics, OKRs, KPIs, lead time, and delivery predictability.
- Stakeholder communication with business, product, executives, clients, partners, and engineering teams.
- Translating business needs into technology solutions.
- Product thinking: roadmap, backlog, MVPs, value prioritization, acceptance criteria, discovery-to-delivery, and outcome metrics.
- AI adoption and implementation: LLMs, agents, ChatGPT, Claude, Copilot, Gemini, n8n, automation, process mapping, quick wins, MVPs, and handoff.
- Digital transformation, change management, innovation radar, and continuous improvement.
- Mentoring, onboarding, leadership development, feedback, 1:1s, PDIs, and building technical maturity.
- Architecture and engineering practices at a leadership level: solution architecture, APIs, cloud, DevOps/DevSecOps concepts, observability, quality, scalability, technical debt, and risk anticipation.
- Community building, enablement, newsletters/blogs, workshops, technical knowledge sharing, and technology culture.

Skills to grow or position carefully:

- Product Owner / Product Manager language for AI products, chatbots, data-driven products, and digital products.
- AI implementation leadership with measurable efficiency gains, adoption, training, and enablement.
- Cloud and DevOps vocabulary where the job requires AWS, GCP, Azure, DevOps, DevSecOps, FinOps, observability, or DORA.
- Budget management, hiring, global team management, and large-scale multi-squad leadership are attractive in roles but should be treated as missing evidence unless the CV or user later confirms them.

Skills to de-emphasize:

- Pure coding execution as the main value proposition.
- Pure administrative project management.
- Pure ML/NLP research depth.

## Strong Fit Pattern

Score a role higher when it combines several of these signals:

- Leads squads, engineering teams, product/technology initiatives, or communities.
- Connects technology work to business strategy, customer value, or operational efficiency.
- Requires agile leadership, delivery metrics, flow improvement, DORA/OKRs/KPIs, or continuous improvement.
- Requires communication with executives, stakeholders, clients, product teams, and engineering teams.
- Includes mentoring, team health, feedback, developing leaders, or technical maturity.
- Includes AI, LLMs, automation, no-code/low-code, agents, prompt work, process mapping, or AI adoption.
- Includes digital transformation, change management, innovation, or technology culture.
- Requires enough technical background to guide architecture, quality, scalability, observability, cloud, APIs, or software delivery without being a pure developer role.

## Possible Fit Pattern

Score as possible fit when:

- The role title is PO, PM, Squad Leader, Technical Expert, or Community Manager, but the content includes technology leadership, product/technology translation, agile delivery, AI, stakeholder influence, or team enablement.
- The role is adjacent to the user's background and could be positioned through transferable leadership, technical communication, and transformation experience.
- A few tool-specific requirements are missing but the core work matches.

## Stretch Pattern

Score as stretch when:

- The role requires strong product management experience as the primary identity, but also includes AI, squads, technical stakeholders, backlog, roadmap, and business-value prioritization.
- The role requires large-scale budget ownership, recruiting ownership, global team management, or multi-squad leadership beyond what the CV explicitly proves.
- The role requires formal Software Engineering education or advanced English beyond the CV evidence.

## Positioning

Preferred positioning:

- Tech Manager who connects engineering execution, people leadership, business strategy, and AI-enabled transformation.
- Engineering leader with hands-on development background, strong agile delivery practice, and stakeholder communication.
- Technology transformation leader focused on measurable outcomes, team maturity, AI adoption, and operational efficiency.
- Product-minded technology leader who can translate ambiguous business needs into prioritized, viable, scalable solutions.
- AI adoption and enablement leader who co-creates solutions with teams, trains users, builds autonomy, and measures impact.

Avoid positioning the user as:

- Only a front-end developer.
- Only a Scrum facilitator.
- Only a project status reporter.
- A deep ML researcher or data scientist.
- A purely administrative PMO profile.
