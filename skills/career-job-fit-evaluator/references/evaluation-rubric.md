# Job Fit Evaluation Rubric

Use a 100-point score, but score conservatively. Do not inflate the match to be pleasant. Give high scores only when the job post has direct evidence of fit across responsibilities, required skills, seniority, target profile, and formal requirements.

## Scoring

- Core responsibility and experience match: 25 points
- Required skills, tools, and technical/domain capabilities: 20 points
- Seniority, leadership scope, and responsibility fit: 15 points
- Target profile alignment: 15 points
- Formal education, credentials, and mandatory background fit: 15 points
- Domain, language, location, work model, and practical constraints: 10 points

## Decision Bands

- 90-100: Strong fit
- 75-89: Possible fit
- 60-74: Stretch
- 0-59: Not recommended

Use `Strong fit` rarely. A role should usually need direct evidence in most major categories and no major hard-requirement gaps to reach 90+.

## Conservative Scoring Rules

- Start from a neutral score and add points for evidence. Do not start high and subtract only obvious gaps.
- Missing evidence should reduce confidence and usually reduce score.
- Transferable experience is valuable, but it should not receive the same score as direct required experience.
- A preferred qualification gap should usually reduce 0-5 points.
- A required qualification gap should usually reduce 8-20 points depending on importance.
- A hard dealbreaker or hard mandatory requirement that is not met should cap the score, even when the rest of the role looks attractive.
- If the job post is vague, avoid a high score; use lower confidence and explain which assumptions would need confirmation.

## Education and Credential Weight

Treat formal education as a meaningful scoring factor when the job explicitly asks for a specific degree, field, registration, license, or academic background.

Evaluation rules:

- If the job asks for technology, software engineering, computer science, information systems, design, product, digital, engineering broadly, exact sciences, or "related areas", treat the user's education plus experience as reasonably adjacent.
- If the job asks for `Software Engineering`, `Computer Science`, `Information Systems`, or similar as a hard requirement, mark it as a screening risk because the CV degree is Industrial Design. Do not automatically reject if experience strongly compensates, but usually cap the score at 82 unless the post accepts equivalent experience or related areas.
- If the job asks for a degree clearly outside technology/design/product/digital/exact sciences, such as health, law, accounting, finance-only, civil engineering, chemical engineering, education/pedagogy, or another regulated/specialized field, apply a stronger penalty.
- If that unrelated degree is mandatory and no equivalent experience is allowed, usually cap the score at 60.
- If that unrelated degree is preferred rather than mandatory, reduce the score but do not cap below `Possible fit` if the role content strongly matches.
- If the role requires a license, professional registration, or certification that the CV does not show, treat it as a hard gap and cap the score according to how essential it is.

## Score Caps

Use these caps before finalizing the score:

- Missing mandatory unrelated degree with no equivalence allowed: max 60.
- Missing mandatory tech/software degree, but strong equivalent experience: max 82.
- Missing mandatory certification/license: max 70, or max 55 if legally/regulatorily required.
- Role is mostly outside the user's target direction: max 65.
- Role is mostly pure hands-on coding without leadership/product/transformation scope: max 70.
- Role is mostly administrative PMO/status reporting: max 68.
- Role requires fluent/native English and the CV only proves B2: max 75 unless the user says they are comfortable.
- Role requires deep ML research/model training as the core work: max 62 unless the post is actually about applied AI adoption/automation.
- Job post is too vague to verify major requirements: max 78 and use `Low` or `Medium` confidence.

## Confidence

Use `High` when the CV, target profile, and job post are all specific.

Use `Medium` when one source is incomplete or the job post is vague.

Use `Low` when the CV or target profile is mostly missing, or the role description lacks requirements.

Confidence is not the same as score. A job can have a decent score with low confidence if important details are missing.

## Rules

- Penalize hard dealbreakers even if the experience score is high.
- Do not penalize missing preferred qualifications as heavily as missing required qualifications.
- Mark missing evidence as `missing evidence`, not as a confirmed lack of ability.
- Identify transferable experience when exact experience is absent.
- Do not recommend applying to roles that violate non-negotiables unless the user explicitly asks for a stretch strategy.
- Be direct when a role looks too junior, too senior, outside the target direction, or likely to waste the user's time.
- Explain why the score is not higher when there are meaningful gaps, especially education, mandatory tools, language, seniority, domain, or legal/regulatory requirements.

## Optional Add-Ons

When useful, include:

- A tailored LinkedIn message to a recruiter
- CV bullet suggestions aligned with the job post
- Interview talking points
- Keywords to add to the user's profile if they are truthful
